Ruitong Tian

PhD student · University of Florida

I am Ruitong Tian, a PhD student in the ICIC Lab at the University of Florida, where I am fortunate to be advised by Prof. Eric Jing Du.

My research focuses on robot learning and dexterous manipulation. I aim to develop methods that enable robots to learn manipulation skills from human demonstrations by drawing on both motion and tactile information. Specifically, I am interested in how human limb coupling and wearable sensing can provide supervision for learning motion and contact from video, and how these representations can support robot policy learning. My recent work also examines how wearable interface design influences the demonstrations we collect and the policies trained on them.

Before joining the University of Florida, I received my master's degree in Civil Engineering from the National University of Singapore and my bachelor's degree in Civil Engineering from South China University of Technology.

ExoBridge: Learning a Bare Hand to Hand-Worn Exoskeleton Mapping through Human Limb Coupling

Ruitong Tian, Xianyao Li, Noah B. Wilson, Fang Xu, and Eric Jing Du

We use paired bimanual demonstrations to connect bare-hand RGB video with the motion and tactile state measured by a sensorized exoskeleton.

Our dataset contains 1,215 demonstrations across four tasks. On held-out collection sessions, we achieve 0.916 contact AUROC.

Paper ↗PDF ↗Research figure ↗
Research details

We train a temporal visual model to predict contact, tactile intensity, and relative encoder motion from video alone. We combine MediaPipe hand crops, frozen DINOv2 visual features, and causal temporal convolutions, and evaluate across four manipulation tasks with collection sessions kept separate.

From Wearable Interfaces to Dexterous Policies: Contact Shifts and Tactile Representations

Ruitong Tian, Fang Xu, Noah B. Wilson, Xianyao Li, and Eric Jing Du

We study how wearable-interface geometry changes the tactile interactions captured in human demonstrations, and how the choice of tactile representation affects the manipulation policies learned from those demonstrations.

We evaluate policies on a 12-DoF XHand + Franka Emika. With richer tactile inputs, pooled policy success increases from 35% to 52%.

Paper ↗PDF ↗Research figure ↗
Research details

We collect wearable demonstrations, compare tactile representations, and train Diffusion Policy. We compare binary aggregate contact with spatial-plus-force tactile inputs, deploying both on a 12-DoF XHand mounted on a Franka Emika arm.

Ergonomics · Accepted October 9, 2026

Semantically Enhanced Eye-Tracking Controls in Human-Robot Interaction: A Comparison Study

Ruitong Tian, Hengxu You, Fang Xu, Jiahao Wu, Bowen Sun, Thanakon Uthai, and Eric Jing Du

We compare gaze, voice, and hand-gesture control of a Franka Emika arm with HoloLens 2 in a 30-participant within-subject study.

We find a mean completion time per step of 17.10 s with gaze, compared with 32.78 s with hand gesture.

Research details & contributions

We combine FastSAM segmentation with Azure Kinect RGB-D target localization in the semantic gaze and voice interfaces, and evaluate task performance, gaze behavior, and NASA-TLX workload. I contributed to methodology, investigation, data curation, formal analysis, and writing.

Under submission · 2026 · Tactile navigation

Nudge Before You Push: Physics-Aware Navigation via Tactile Probing

Xianyao Li, Fang Xu, Ruitong Tian, Bowen Sun, Xiao Hu, Yang Ye, and Jing Du

We use a brief nudge to probe obstructing containers. In TANav, TacPhys reads the resulting force sequences to estimate container mass and inform whether to push or detour.

In simulation, we reduce missed pushes from 28.7% with mean peak force to 5.5% with TacPhys at comparable low-risk operating points.

Paper ↗PDF ↗Research figure ↗
Research details

We evaluate repeated-patrol planning in simulation. In separate hardware studies, we assess offline mass transfer and calibration: the calibrated boundary yields 93.5% pooled offline accuracy. A distinct raw-peak rule gives 15 of 20 correct online decisions on unseen boxes.
